◆ ABOUT THIS TOOL

What Is Keyword Density?

How often a word or phrase appears compared with the total number of words.

Keyword density is the number of times a keyword appears in a text, divided by the total number of words, as a percentage. If “tea” appears 12 times in a 600-word article, its density is 12 ÷ 600 = 2%.

This checker uses that same formula for phrases: a 2- or 3-word phrase that appears 6 times in 600 words has a density of 1%. Phrases are only counted inside a sentence — never across full stops, commas or line breaks — so the results are real phrases from your text.

There is no ideal density. Google doesn't use a target percentage; it warns against keyword stuffing — repeating words unnaturally. Use the numbers to spot over-used words and to check that your main topic is actually covered, not to hit a number.

Any language. Words are detected with Unicode rules, so Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Arabic, Russian and other languages work. Chinese, Japanese and Thai, which don't put spaces between words, are split into words with your browser's built-in word segmenter. The stop-word list is English.

✓ HOW TO READ IT

Using the Results

Numbers are a guide, not a goal.

1

Your topic should lead

The top 2- and 3-word phrases should describe what the page is about. If they don't, the page may be unfocused.

2

Watch for stuffing

A word far ahead of everything else (often above ~3%) can read as unnatural. Use synonyms and related terms.

3

Check the focus keyword

Make sure it appears — especially in the title, the first paragraph and a heading — without forcing it.

4

Write for people

Search engines understand synonyms and context. Clear, helpful writing beats any density target.
