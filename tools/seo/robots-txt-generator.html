    ◆ ABOUT THIS TOOL

    What Is robots.txt?

    A plain-text file that tells crawlers which parts of your site they may fetch.

    robots.txt lives at the root of a site (https://example.com/robots.txt) and follows the Robots Exclusion Protocol (RFC 9309). It contains groups of rules: each group names one or more crawlers with User-agent and lists paths they may not (Disallow) or may (Allow) crawl. It can also point crawlers to your Sitemap.

    Important: robots.txt controls crawling, not indexing. A blocked page can still appear in Google if other sites link to it — to keep a page out of search results, use a noindex meta tag and let Google crawl it. And robots.txt is public and voluntary: never use it to hide private content.

    AI crawlers in 2026. Most AI companies now run separate crawlers for model training (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot…) and for search and answers (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot…). The “Block AI training” policy blocks the first kind while keeping your site visible in AI search and answer engines. Blocking Google-Extended doesn't affect Google Search.

    A crawler obeys only the most specific group that matches it. If you add a group for Googlebot, Googlebot ignores the User-agent: * rules entirely — the generator warns you when that drops a rule you probably wanted.

    ✓ SYNTAX

    How Google Reads robots.txt

    The matching rules the built-in tester uses.

    UA

    One group per crawler

    The group with the most specific matching User-agent wins; groups aren't combined. * is used only if no other group matches. User-agent names are case-insensitive.

    /

    Longest rule wins

    When several rules match a URL, the longest path wins. If an Allow and a Disallow are equally long, Allow wins. Paths are case-sensitive and start with /.

    *$

    Wildcards

    * matches any sequence of characters; $ marks the end of the URL. /*.pdf$ blocks every URL ending in .pdf.

    !

    Not supported by Google

    Crawl-delay, Noindex and Host are ignored by Google. Files over 500 KiB are cut off.
