    What Is the Open Graph Protocol?

    The tags that turn a bare link into a rich, clickable preview card.

    Open Graph (og:) meta tags were created by Facebook and are now read by almost every app that shows link previews: LinkedIn, X, WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, iMessage, Pinterest and more. Without them, platforms guess — often picking the wrong image or a random sentence.

    Four properties are required by the protocol: og:title, og:type, og:image and og:url. Adding og:description, og:site_name and image dimensions makes previews faster and more reliable.

    X (Twitter) reads Open Graph tags as a fallback, but it needs its own twitter:card tag to choose the card layout — a large image card or a compact summary card with a square thumbnail.

    Platforms cache previews. After changing tags on a live page, use the Facebook Sharing Debugger or LinkedIn Post Inspector to force a refresh. Preview images in this tool load directly from your URL, so they only appear for publicly reachable images.

    The URL must be absolute (starting with https://), publicly accessible without a login, and point directly to an image file. Some hosts block hot-linking. If it fails here, it will usually fail on social platforms too.

    X falls back to og:title, og:description and og:image, but you need twitter:card to get a card at all. twitter:site and twitter:creator are optional attribution.
