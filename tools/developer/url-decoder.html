◆ ABOUT THIS TOOL

What Does a URL Decoder Do?

It reverses percent-encoding so you can read what a link really contains.

Browsers, servers and analytics tools percent-encode URLs: spaces become %20 or +, slashes become %2F, and non-English characters become sequences of UTF-8 bytes like %E6%97%A5. Decoding turns them back into readable text.

That's essential when debugging redirects and OAuth callbacks, reading UTM-tagged links, inspecting server logs or extracting the real destination hidden inside a tracking URL.

Real-world links are often messy. Some are double-encoded (%2520 instead of %20) because they passed through two systems — Decode repeatedly unwraps up to five layers and tells you how many it found.

Others contain a stray % or an invalid byte sequence. Instead of failing like decodeURIComponent, this tool decodes everything that is valid, leaves the broken parts untouched and warns you about them.

⚠ TROUBLESHOOTING

Common URL Decoding Problems

What you might see, and what it means.

%25

Double encoding: %2520

%25 is an encoded %, so %2520 decodes to %20 and then to a space. Enable Decode repeatedly to unwrap every layer automatically.

+

Plus signs

In query strings + means a space. In paths and some APIs it's a literal plus. If your result has unexpected spaces, turn off + as space. A real plus is always encoded as %2B.

%?

Malformed sequences

A % not followed by two hex digits (like 100%) or bytes that aren't valid UTF-8 (like %E9 from old Latin-1 systems) are left as-is and reported in a warning.
