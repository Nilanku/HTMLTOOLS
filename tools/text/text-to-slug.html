◆ ABOUT THIS TOOL

What Is a URL Slug?

The readable part of a URL that identifies a page — like /blog/text-to-slug-guide.

A slug is the last part of a URL, written in a URL-safe form: lowercase words separated by hyphens, without spaces, punctuation or special characters. Good slugs are short, readable and describe the page, which helps both visitors and search engines.

This generator converts any text into a slug: it lowercases letters, replaces spaces and punctuation with a separator, turns & into “and”, removes apostrophes without splitting words (don't → dont) and collapses repeated separators.

Accents and other languages. With Latin only on, accented letters are transliterated (é → e, ß → ss, ø → o) and characters from other scripts are dropped. Turn it off to create Unicode slugs that keep words in Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Arabic, Chinese or any language — browsers show them as text, and they're sent percent-encoded.

If a title has no Latin letters at all (for example, a Hindi title) and Latin only is on, the slug would be empty — the tool tells you so instead of silently producing nothing.

✓ BEST PRACTICES

How to Write Good Slugs

Google recommends simple, descriptive URLs.

-

Use hyphens

Google recommends hyphens to separate words: red-shoes, not red_shoes or redshoes.

a

Lowercase

URLs are case-sensitive on most servers. Lowercase slugs avoid duplicate URLs like /Page and /page.

✂

Short & descriptive

Keep the words that describe the page. Removing stop words and setting a max length helps.

↪

Don't change live slugs

Changing the slug of a published page changes its URL. If you must, add a 301 redirect from the old URL.
