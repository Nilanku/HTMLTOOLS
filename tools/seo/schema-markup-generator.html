    What Is Schema Markup?

    Structured data that tells search engines exactly what a page is about.

    Schema markup is code — usually JSON-LD inside a <script type="application/ld+json"> tag — that describes the content of a page using the shared schema.org vocabulary: a product and its price, an article and its author, a business and its opening hours. Search engines use it to understand pages and to show rich results.

    This page generates two common types. WebSite markup on your home page helps Google choose the site name shown in search results. Person markup describes a person — ideally inside ProfilePage markup on an about-me or author page.

    For other types, use the dedicated generators — each one knows Google's rules for its type: FAQ, Article, Breadcrumb, Product, Local Business and Organization.

    Note: the sitelinks search box that WebSite markup with SearchAction used to trigger was retired by Google in November 2024, so this generator doesn't add it.

    Which Schema Type Do You Need?

    Pick the type that matches the main content of the page.

    WebSite — on this page

    Home page only. Gives Google your preferred site name (and alternates) for search results.

    👤

    Person — on this page

    About-me, author and team-member pages. Use ProfilePage when the whole page is about that person.

    🏢

    Organization · Local Business

    Your company (logo, profiles, identifiers) — or a physical location with address and opening hours.

    📄

    Article · Product · FAQ · Breadcrumb

    Blog posts and news, product pages with price and reviews, Q&A content, and the page's position in your site.
