◆ ABOUT THIS TOOL

What Is Glassmorphism?

A UI style that makes panels look like frosted glass floating above a colourful background.

Glassmorphism combines four ingredients: a semi-transparent background, a background blur (with backdrop-filter: blur()), a subtle light border that catches the “edge” of the glass, and a soft shadow that lifts the panel off the page.

backdrop-filter applies effects to whatever is behind the element — unlike filter, which affects the element itself. Adding saturate() makes the colours bleeding through more vivid, which is what gives the effect its glow.

The effect only works when there's something interesting behind the glass: gradients, shapes or photos. On a flat, single-colour background, glass just looks like a faded box — that's why this preview uses colourful scenes and lets you try your own image.

Keep text readable: frosted glass lowers contrast. Use a higher opacity or blur behind body text, keep important text large and bold, and check contrast over the busiest part of your background.

📐 PROPERTIES

The CSS Behind Frosted Glass

What each generated declaration does.

B

background: rgba(255,255,255,.2)

A translucent tint. White gives classic frosted glass; dark tints suit dark themes. 10–30% opacity is typical.

F

backdrop-filter: blur(12px)

Blurs everything behind the element. 8–20px is the sweet spot; more blur means better text readability.

S

saturate(160%)

Boosts the colours seen through the glass so it looks lit from behind rather than greyed out.

W

-webkit-backdrop-filter

The prefixed version for Safari before 18 and older iOS devices. Always include it alongside the standard property.

E

border: 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,.3)

A faint light edge that defines the pane — the detail that makes glass look like glass.

?

@supports not (backdrop-filter: …)

A fallback that makes the background more opaque in browsers without backdrop-filter, so text stays readable.
